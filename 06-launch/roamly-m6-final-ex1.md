### Deliverable 2 · Competitive Intel & Strategic Bet (Module 2)

**Strategic bet**

I am betting on the organizer of a friend group who has already chosen an experience, because at that moment Viator can only charge one card and a payment pool cannot hold a seat. Only a marketplace whose hosts have agreed to hold seats can confirm the whole group at once, and Viator cannot copy that without renegotiating its supply.

### Deliverable 3 · Positioning (Module 3)

**Positioning statement**

For the friend who organizes the group trip and ends up fronting the money and chasing everyone to pay them back, Roamly Groups is the group checkout for hand-picked local experiences that lets each friend pay for their own seat while the host holds the seats, so the group books together or not at all. Unlike booking on one card and collecting on Venmo, or a marketplace that holds the seats but still charges one person, Roamly Groups never puts the trip on anyone's card.

**One-liner**

Share one link, everyone pays their own seat, and the group books together or nobody is charged.

### Deliverable 5 · Pricing Recommendation (Module 5)

**Model & packaging**

Transaction-based: the host pays through the commission Roamly already takes, and travelers never see a fee or a tier. Three host tiers: Group-ready (no uplift, the launch tier), Group Plus (+4 points on group bookings, the default, pays for itself at one extra seat every 3.8 groups), Private Groups (+6 points).

**Research to validate**

Van Westendorp with hosts on the Group Plus uplift, run only after they have seen group bookings, then Max-Diff on tier contents. The assumption that matters most comes first: that hosts keep holding seats after living with holds, tested with a one-quarter pilot in the first launch city.

### Deliverable 6 · Individual Insights

**Friction points, what was hardest, and how I worked through it**

Numbers I could not source. The scenario gives no data, and every figure I wanted either came from AI output I had to check or had to be derived from the structure. Two AI-drafted claims turned out wrong on inspection: a 38% statistic cited to the wrong page, and a price threshold nobody could source. I worked through it by recomputing every number and verifying every citation before it went into a deliverable, and by labeling the one invented figure as illustrative.

**Biggest "aha" moment**

The product is a supply policy, not a checkout screen. I started in Module 1 thinking about the organizer's pain. Competitive research showed that Airbnb built this exact feature in 2017 and killed it because hosts hated holding seats. From then on, the hold became the bet, the core product truth, the thing the asset had to show, and the reason the host pays in the pricing.

**Key takeaways**

Every downstream decision is only as good as the upstream one it traces to. Pricing fell out of the bet: once the hold was the moat, the host was the obvious payer. I also learned to cold-read everything. The four blind reads of one social post each found a different flaw, including a statistic that contradicted the product's own mechanic.
