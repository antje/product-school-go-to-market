For people who organize trips for their friends and end up fronting the money and chasing everyone to pay them back, Roamly Groups is a group checkout that holds the slot while every friend pays their own share. Unlike a group chat plus a payment app, the booking confirms for everyone at once and nobody is out of pocket.

The goal

Expand

Recover the group bookings that today start and die. Launch to existing organizers; win completed group bookings with several seats each.

Not launching against: new-user signups (tracked as by-product), company or team bookings, paid acquisition.

Who we reach, who we convert

We reach

The organizer

Existing Roamly user, 2+ bookings in 12 months, with a group signal: same-slot sessions, split-pay tickets, "book for my friends" feedback. In the cities where failed group sessions cluster.

Pain: unpaid coordination work and carrying the group's money.

We convert

The friends

The 2 to 7 people the organizer invites. Mostly not on Roamly. Each one must complete before the booking exists, so their path decides completion rate.

Pain today: none. Venmo works fine for them. We have to beat Venmo on speed.

Key messages

Organizer

Never front the money again.

Pick the experience, invite the group, and each friend pays their own seat. No spreadsheet, no reminders, no IOUs.

Friend

One tap and your seat is yours.

Pay your share from the link. No app to install, no account to create before you pay, nobody chasing you.

Both

Confirmed together or charged never.

Roamly holds the slot while the group pays. If the group does not complete, nobody is charged and nobody is stuck.

Channels

Primary
In product."Booking for a group?" at checkout for segment organizers in launch cities. Post-booking prompt on the same-slot pattern. Direct email to the split-pay ticket cohort.
The loop
Invite link.SMS or shared link, guest pay on one screen, account offered after payment. This step decides completion rate and gets the design and test budget.
Seeding
Where organizers already talk.Trip-planning creators and travel communities in launch cities. "Booked as a group of 6" on share and review surfaces.
Not now
Paid acquisition, team and company bookings, itinerary as headline.

Success metrics

Leading
Group completion rate.Started group bookings that reach fully paid confirmation. Baseline: current drop-off on same-slot sessions; target is a lift over it once measured. Provisional bar: at least 80% of the individual booking completion rate, because a group has one more failure point, the last friend.
Success
Seats per booking among segment organizers.Target: at least 4 seats per group booking on average, read against a holdout of organizers who never see the entry point. Groups of 3 to 8 have a midpoint of 5.5; 4 leaves room for the last friend dropping out.
By-product
Invited-friend signups and their 90-day second booking.Measured, not targeted.
Guardrails
Held slots released unpaid and invite-to-confirmation time.Released slots stay below the cost the supply side agreed to. Invite-to-confirmation stays in hours, not days.

Launch requirements

  • Slot hold for the payment window. Without it the organizer fronts money or loses the slot, and the value prop is false.
  • Guest pay for friends, one screen, account creation after payment.
  • Split pay leads the message. Itinerary planning ships in the product, not in the headline.
  • City gate on the entry point so the launch reads clean in the launch cities before it goes wide.

Open before launch

  • Hold window. How long, and who eats the cost when a held slot is released unpaid.
  • Friend screen. What one-screen guest pay looks like. Test it with 20 organizers in one launch city; if per-friend completion is below 90%, redesign before going wide (at 80% per friend a group of seven completes about 21% of the time; at 95%, about 70%).
  • The numbers. Current drop-off rate and organizer count in the launch cities, so target and ceiling are written down.
