# Roamly Groups · Positioning Framework & Statement, Module 3

## Target Persona

**Who they are**

The organizer: the one friend in a group of 3 to 8 who plans the trip. They have booked on Roamly before, usually more than once. They are the person who says "I'll book it" and then carries everything: the shortlist, the group chat, the card, the reminders.

**What they're trying to accomplish**

Get the whole group into one experience they have already picked, with everyone committed and paid, without becoming the bank or the collections agent. The outcome is a confirmed booking for eight people that cost them no money up front and no chasing afterwards.

**What's getting in their way**

Every marketplace charges one card. So they either front $500 or more for people who have not said yes, or they wait for everyone to commit and watch the slot sell out. Group chat plus Venmo works for the friends, who have no pain, but it leaves the organizer holding the risk and doing unpaid coordination work. Roughly one in three group travelers has not been paid back in full.

## Positioning Framework

**FOR (Target Audience)**

The organizer: the one friend in a group of 3 to 8 who plans the trip, has booked on Roamly before, and ends up fronting the money and chasing everyone to pay them back. They have already picked or shortlisted an experience in one of our cities. What they need now is for the group to commit.

**WHO WANT/NEED (The Need)**

"I just want everyone to pay for their own spot so I'm not the bank. I don't want to put $600 on my card for people who haven't said yes, and I don't want to spend the trip reminding friends to Venmo me."

**WE OFFER (The Offering)**

Roamly Groups is a group checkout for local experiences. The organizer picks the experience and shares one link. Each friend pays for their own seat from the link, with no account needed. Roamly holds the seats while the group pays, and the booking confirms for everyone at once when the last seat is paid.

**BECAUSE OF (Value Proposition)**

The organizer is never the bank. Nobody fronts the money, nobody chases anyone, and nobody is charged unless the whole group is in. The group actually books instead of stalling in the chat.

**DESPITE (Barriers)**

Roamly is in 40 cities, not everywhere, and most people find experiences on Viator or Airbnb first. The friends have no pain today: Venmo already works for them, so the payment link has to be faster than what they do now. And the hold has a deadline: if the group does not fill in time, the seats are released.

**BETTER THAN (Differentiation)**

Viator's Reserve Now & Pay Later holds the seats but still charges one card; a payment pool like Cash App Pools collects money but cannot hold a seat; Airbnb tried group payments and dropped them. Roamly Groups is the only one where vetted experts have agreed to hold seats for a group, so everyone pays their own share and the booking confirms together or not at all.

## Positioning Statement

> Roamly Groups is a group checkout for local experiences: the organizer shares one link, each friend pays for their own seat, and Roamly holds the seats until the whole group has paid. Nobody fronts the money, nobody chases anyone, and the trip books for everyone at once or not at all.

## Pressure Test

**Test run**

Competitor Discomfort, could a competitor use it word-for-word?

**What it revealed**

Viator could use the statement word for word: swap the name and it reads as a release note for Reserve Now & Pay Later. "Group checkout for local experiences" is a category, and "experiences" is Viator's word, so the frame favors the 300,000-listing catalogue. "Nobody fronts the money" is already half of Pay Later's pitch, because Viator takes no money down at booking. The three mechanics (one link, own seat, held seats) are buildable by any marketplace with a payments team. The one thing Viator cannot say, that the host running the experience has agreed to hold seats for a group, was in the Better Than component but never made it into the statement. The statement also lost the organizer persona.

**Blind-read result**

A stranger described it as "a way to reserve an activity for a bunch of people where each person covers their own share directly, instead of one person covering everyone." For: "the person in a friend circle who always does the planning, fronts the cash, and nags everyone for weeks to get repaid." Why switch: "no single person is out the money, no reminders needed, and the planner is never stuck having reserved for eight when only five committed." Two soft spots, both fixed in the final version: "expert" read as possibly a Roamly staff role rather than the host, and the reader's first question was what happens if one person never pays.

## Final Positioning Statement

> Roamly Groups is a group checkout for Roamly's hand-picked local experiences: the organizer shares one link, each friend pays for their own seat, and the host holds the seats until the whole group has paid or the deadline passes. Nobody puts the trip on their card, nobody chases anyone, and the group books together or not at all.
