## Responses

### Step 1 · Generate your positioning statement

**Prompt**

I have completed a positioning framework for a new feature called Roamly Groups, a coordinated group booking feature on a curated travel experience platform. Here are my six components:

FOR (Target Audience): The organizer: the one friend in a group of 3 to 8 who plans the trip, has booked on Roamly before, and ends up fronting the money and chasing everyone to pay them back. They have already picked or shortlisted an experience in one of our cities. What they need now is for the group to commit.

WHO WANT/NEED (The Need): "I just want everyone to pay for their own spot so I'm not the bank. I don't want to put $600 on my card for people who haven't said yes, and I don't want to spend the trip reminding friends to Venmo me."

WE OFFER (The Offering): Roamly Groups is a group checkout for local experiences. The organizer picks the experience and shares one link. Each friend pays for their own seat from the link, with no account needed. Roamly holds the seats while the group pays, and the booking confirms for everyone at once when the last seat is paid.

BECAUSE OF (Value Proposition): The organizer is never the bank. Nobody fronts the money, nobody chases anyone, and nobody is charged unless the whole group is in. The group actually books instead of stalling in the chat.

DESPITE (Barriers): Roamly is in 40 cities, not everywhere, and most people find experiences on Viator or Airbnb first. The friends have no pain today: Venmo already works for them, so the payment link has to be faster than what they do now. And the hold has a deadline: if the group does not fill in time, the seats are released.

BETTER THAN (Differentiation): Viator's Reserve Now & Pay Later holds the seats but still charges one card; a payment pool like Cash App Pools collects money but cannot hold a seat; Airbnb tried group payments and dropped them. Roamly Groups is the only one where vetted experts have agreed to hold seats for a group, so everyone pays their own share and the booking confirms together or not at all.

Using these inputs, write a sharp positioning statement of one to two sentences that leads with the core product offering and then follows with the core value proposition. Keep it specific, clear, and free of jargon.

**Response**

Positioning statement for Roamly Groups (offering first, value proposition second):

"Roamly Groups is a group checkout for local experiences: the organizer shares one link, each friend pays for their own seat, and Roamly holds the seats until the whole group has paid. Nobody fronts the money, nobody chases anyone, and the trip books for everyone at once or not at all."

Rationale:
- Sentence one is offering only: category (group checkout for local experiences) plus the three mechanics that matter (one link, own seat, held seats). "No account needed" omitted as a detail, not the position.
- Sentence two is the value proposition in the organizer's words. "Nobody fronts the money, nobody chases anyone" maps to the two halves of the need ($600 on the card, chasing Venmo). "Books for everyone at once or not at all" carries the differentiation without naming competitors: it rules out Viator (one card) and Cash App Pools (no seat hold) in one clause.
- No jargon (no "seamless", "coordinated", "platform", "solution").

One-sentence alternate for a slide:
"Roamly Groups is a group checkout for local experiences where the organizer shares one link, every friend pays for their own seat, and Roamly holds the seats until the whole group is in, so nobody is the bank and the trip books together or not at all."

Note: the "vetted experts agreed to hold seats" proof point works better as the first supporting line under the statement than inside it; if it must go in, it replaces "Roamly holds the seats" with "the expert holds the seats" in sentence one.

### Step 2 · Pressure test B, competitor discomfort (as Viator)

**Prompt**

Now role-play as a product leader at Viator, the Tripadvisor-owned tours and activities marketplace with 300,000+ listings and the Reserve Now & Pay Later feature that holds a reservation with no money down and charges one card two days before. Read the following positioning statement for Roamly Groups. Could your company use this statement word for word without anyone noticing? If yes, what specifically makes it too generic? If no, what makes it ownable to Roamly specifically?

"Roamly Groups is a group checkout for local experiences: the organizer shares one link, each friend pays for their own seat, and Roamly holds the seats until the whole group has paid. Nobody fronts the money, nobody chases anyone, and the trip books for everyone at once or not at all."

**Response**

Viator product leader role-play: could Viator use the Roamly Groups statement word for word?

VERDICT: Yes, with one search-and-replace. Swap "Roamly" for "Viator" and it reads as the next release note for Reserve Now & Pay Later ("RNPL: Split Pay"). Viator already holds the seat with no money down; splitting the charge across cards is a payments feature (Stripe/Adyen sell it), not a supply feature.

WHAT MAKES IT GENERIC:
1. "Group checkout for local experiences" is a category, not a position. "Experiences" is Viator's word; they have 300k, Roamly has 40 cities. The frame favors the bigger catalogue.
2. The three mechanics (one link, own seat, held seats) are buildable by any marketplace with a payments team; none depend on how Roamly sources supply.
3. "Nobody fronts the money" is already half of RNPL's pitch (no money down at booking). The statement never says what is different: no single card is ever charged for the group.
4. "Books together or not at all" is the sharpest clause but is a policy, not a moat; Viator could add a fill deadline to RNPL.

WHAT VIATOR CANNOT SAY (and the statement leaves out):
- The hold. RNPL is supplier opt-in, hold windows vary, and Viator cannot make 300k independent operators agree to hold 6 seats for a week for a group that may not fill. Roamly can, because supply is small, curated, and the experts agreed directly. This is in the BETTER THAN component but never made it into the statement.
- The organizer persona ("the friend who plans the trip and ends up being the bank"). Viator books anyone, anything; naming one person is off-brand for a catalogue.

REVISED STATEMENT VIATOR COULD NOT LIFT:
"Roamly Groups is a group checkout for Roamly's hand-picked local experiences: the organizer shares one link, each friend pays for their own seat, and the expert running the experience holds the seats until the whole group has paid. Nobody puts the trip on their card, nobody chases anyone, and the group books together or not at all."

Three changes: "hand-picked" names the supply model; "the expert running the experience holds the seats" moves the hold from platform to a person who agreed to it (unpromisable across a marketplace); "nobody puts the trip on their card" is the direct contrast with RNPL's one-card charge, replacing a line Viator could already claim.

Original statement (from prior task) stands as the clean version; the revised one is recommended if the goal is ownability.

### Blind read · Test C, stranger clarity, on the refined statement

**Prompt**

Drop the Viator role. Now role-play as someone who has never heard of Roamly or Roamly Groups and knows nothing about the conversation above. Read the following positioning statement and explain back in your own words: what is this product, who is it for, and why would someone choose it over the alternatives? Do not use any words from the statement itself.

"Roamly Groups is a group checkout for Roamly's hand-picked local experiences: the organizer shares one link, each friend pays for their own seat, and the expert running the experience holds the seats until the whole group has paid. Nobody puts the trip on their card, nobody chases anyone, and the group books together or not at all."

**Response**

Cold-read role-play (no prior knowledge of Roamly, no words reused from the statement):

WHAT IT IS: A way to reserve an activity (tour, class, tasting) for a bunch of people at once, where each person covers their own share directly with the company instead of one person covering everyone. Roamly reads as a company that sells vetted activities in various cities. The planner sends one web address, everyone opens it and settles up for their own spot, the host keeps the spots reserved meanwhile, and once the last person has settled up the reservation confirms for everyone in one go.

WHO IT IS FOR: The person in a friend circle who always does the planning, fronts the cash, and nags everyone for weeks to get repaid. That person is the buyer; the others are participants.

WHY OVER ALTERNATIVES: Today one person reserves on their own credit and collects afterward via Venmo. This removes both halves: no single person is out the money, no reminders needed. The all-or-nothing rule means the planner is never stuck having reserved for eight when only five committed. Versus a marketplace that reserves but still charges one account, or a shared money pot that collects but cannot reserve, this does both.

WHAT WAS UNCLEAR (cold-reader flags):
1. "Expert" caused a pause: assumed it is the guide/host who leads the activity, but not certain on first read whether it was a person or a Roamly staff role. Consider "host" or "guide" if that is what it means.
2. No mention of what happens if one person never settles up. Presumably spots are released after a deadline, but the statement does not say; first question a reader asks.
3. "Hand-picked" read as a quality signal (company curates its catalogue) but gave no sense of how selective.

Comprehension verdict: the product, the buyer, and the reason to switch all came through without help. The two soft spots are the word "expert" and the missing deadline.

### Step 3 · Refine and save

**Final positioning statement**

> Roamly Groups is a group checkout for Roamly's hand-picked local experiences: the organizer shares one link, each friend pays for their own seat, and the host holds the seats until the whole group has paid or the deadline passes. Nobody puts the trip on their card, nobody chases anyone, and the group books together or not at all.

Changes after the tests: "hand-picked" names the supply model; the hold moved from Roamly to the host who agreed to it; "nobody puts the trip on their card" replaced "nobody fronts the money", which Viator can already claim; "expert" became "host" and the deadline was added after the blind read.
