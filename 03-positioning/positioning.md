# Positioning Framework & Statement: Roamly Groups

> Module 3 · Master Product Positioning · Deliverable 3
>
> The six-component positioning framework for Roamly Groups, the persona it is built for, the statement distilled from it, and the pressure tests that rewrote the statement before release.

## 1. Positioning framework (six components)

The class framework runs For, Who Want/Need, We Offer, Because Of, Despite, Better Than. It is written out in that order, then mapped into the template's six rows.

**For.** The organizer: the one friend in a group of 3 to 8 who plans the trip, has booked on Roamly before, and ends up fronting the money and chasing everyone to pay them back. They have already picked or shortlisted an experience in one of our cities. What they need now is for the group to commit.

**Who want / need.** "I just want everyone to pay for their own spot so I'm not the bank. I don't want to put $600 on my card for people who haven't said yes, and I don't want to spend the trip reminding friends to Venmo me."

**We offer.** A group checkout for local experiences. The organizer picks the experience and shares one link. Each friend pays for their own seat from the link, with no account needed. The expert holds the seats while the group pays, and the booking confirms for everyone at once when the last seat is paid.

**Because of.** The organizer is never the bank. Nobody fronts the money, nobody chases anyone, and nobody is charged unless the whole group is in. The group actually books instead of stalling in the chat.

**Despite.** Roamly is in 40 cities, not everywhere, and most people find experiences on Viator or Airbnb first. The friends have no pain today: Venmo already works for them, so the payment link has to be faster than what they do now. And the hold has a deadline: if the group does not fill in time, the seats are released.

**Better than.** Viator's Reserve Now & Pay Later holds the seats but still charges one card. A payment pool like Cash App Pools collects money but cannot hold a seat. Airbnb tried group payments in 2017 and dropped them in 2018. Roamly Groups is the only one where vetted experts have agreed to hold seats for a group, so everyone pays their own share and the booking confirms together or not at all.

## 2. Core product truth

The expert running the experience has agreed to hold the seats until the whole group has paid.

In customer-facing copy the expert is "the host," because a cold read of "expert" paused on whether it meant a Roamly staff role.

Everything else follows from it: each friend can pay separately, nobody is charged if the group does not fill, and the booking confirms for everyone at once. Neither a bigger marketplace nor a payment app can say it. Viator would have to renegotiate hold rules with hundreds of thousands of operators, and a pool holds money, not inventory. For the two audiences, the organizer reads it as "I am never the bank" and the expert reads it as "the group commits or my seats come back."

## 3. Positioning statement

> For **the friend who organizes the group trip** and **ends up fronting the money and chasing everyone to pay them back**, **Roamly Groups** is the **group checkout for hand-picked local experiences** that **lets each friend pay for their own seat while the host holds the seats, so the group books together or not at all**. Unlike **booking on one card and collecting on Venmo, or a marketplace that holds the seats but still charges one person**, Roamly Groups **never puts the trip on anyone's card and never charges anyone unless the whole group is in**.

In the class's short form, offering first, value second:

> Roamly Groups is a group checkout for Roamly's hand-picked local experiences: the organizer shares one link, each friend pays for their own seat, and the host holds the seats until the whole group has paid or the deadline passes. Nobody puts the trip on their card, nobody chases anyone, and the group books together or not at all.

This is the second version. The first draft was: "Roamly Groups is a group checkout for local experiences: the organizer shares one link, each friend pays for their own seat, and Roamly holds the seats until the whole group has paid. Nobody fronts the money, nobody chases anyone, and the trip books for everyone at once or not at all." The pressure tests below rewrote it.

## 4. Pressure tests

| Test | Pass? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownable**: could a competitor say the same? | Failed first, passes now | Read as a Viator product leader, the first draft could be lifted with one name swap: "nobody fronts the money" is already half of Reserve Now & Pay Later's pitch, "experiences" is Viator's word, and the three mechanics are buildable by any payments team. What Viator cannot say is that the expert agreed to hold the seats. Fixed by naming the supply ("hand-picked"), moving the hold from Roamly to the host, and replacing "nobody fronts the money" with "nobody puts the trip on their card," which contrasts directly with a one-card charge. |
| **Believable**: is the proof real? | Pass, with one open item | The demand signal (tickets, same-slot accounts, drop-off) is in the scenario. The 38% figure is from Airbnb's own 2017 survey. The Viator and Airbnb facts were verified in Module 2. The one claim the product has to earn is "the host holds the seats": it is true only where experts have opted in, which is why the Module 2 bet gates launch cities on a 60% opt-in rate. |
| **Relevant**: does the ICP care? | Pass | A cold reader described the target as "the person in a friend circle who always does the planning, fronts the cash, and nags everyone for weeks to get repaid," without using any of our words, and named the value as "no single person is out the money, no reminders needed, never stuck having reserved for eight when only five committed." The friends are not the ICP and have no pain, and the statement does not claim otherwise. |
| **Durable**: holds as the product grows? | Pass, with a drift watch | The statement rests on the hold rather than a feature list, so adding itinerary planning or wishlists later does not change it. It breaks if the audience widens: once it says "groups" instead of "the organizer," or "travelers" instead of "friends," drift has begun. The check for every later launch is the class's question: does this serve the organizer we positioned for, or expand the definition? |

Two soft spots from the cold read were fixed in the final statement: "expert" read as possibly a Roamly staff role, so it became "host," and the reader's first question was what happens if one person never pays, so "or the deadline passes" was added.

## 5. Positioning vs. messaging note

The positioning is fixed: the host holds the seats so the group books together or not at all, and nobody puts the trip on their card. The emphasis moves by surface.

- **Homepage headline** (organizer, first visit, ten seconds): *Never be the bank for the group trip again.* Subline: pick the experience, share one link, everyone pays their own seat.
- **Sales opener** (expert, in person, the supply-side pitch): *A group of eight books your Saturday slot and you never hold seats for people who will not show. They pay their own way, you get a confirmed group or your seats back by the deadline.*
- **App store blurb** (stranger, two lines): *Book local experiences with your friends. Share a link, everyone pays for their own spot, and the booking confirms for the whole group at once.*
- **Invite link** (friend, one screen, no pain to sell against): *Your seat on the Saturday food tour is held. Pay your share to lock it in.* The organizer's problem is left out, because the friend does not have it.
