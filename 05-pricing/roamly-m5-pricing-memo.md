# Roamly Groups pricing: hosts pay for full groups, travelers never pay extra

**Recommendation.** Charge the host through the commission Roamly already takes. Travelers pay no extra fee and see no tiers. Hosts choose one of three tiers. Revenue grows because group sessions that die today start to complete. No existing price goes up.

## Why this model

- **Value exists only when a whole group pays.** A commission on completed group bookings charges at that moment and at no other.
- **The traveler side is not ready to be priced.** Group completion is the unproven step. A price aimed at people who have not reached the value is the wrong lever.
- **Rejected:** an organizer subscription (organizers book a group two or three times a year); per-seat pricing (it taxes the extra seats we want); a per-seat fee for travelers. Every friend has to pay before the booking exists. If a fee cut each friend's completion from 95% to 90%, a group of seven would complete 48% of the time instead of 70% (0.90^7 vs 0.95^7). Viator and a payment pool cost the traveler nothing.

## The tiers (hosts only)

| Tier | Commission on group bookings | What the host gets |
|---|---|---|
| Group-ready (Good) | 20%, no uplift | Accepts the standard group hold and can take group bookings. No extra charge, because each city needs 60% of hosts to opt in. |
| **Group Plus (Better, the default)** | 24% (+4 pts) | Priority placement in the group entry point and group search, a group-friendly badge, and a group price the host sets for parties of 6+ |
| Private Groups (Best) | 26% (+6 pts) | Everything in Plus, a private-session listing with a minimum group size, and a monthly group demand report |

Split payment and the hold policy are the same in every tier. The hold is the core of the product, so it is never sold as an upsell.

## Finance: revenue comes from volume, not a higher rate

- **Per group:** five seats at $60 is $300 of bookings. Roamly earns $60 at 20%, $72 on Plus and $78 on Private Groups. Today that session earns $0.
- **New cost:** four extra card transactions, about $1.20 per group of five, 2% of the commission. Roamly absorbs it: $58.80 net on Group-ready.
- **Forecast chain:** group sessions started × completion rate × seats × seat price × commission. Two inputs are open: Roamly's real commission rate (20% assumes parity with Viator's base) and group sessions started per launch city. Both must be filled before a forecast leaves this page.
- **No price risk to the core business.** No existing price changes.
- **Rollback threshold:** a Plus host breaks even at one extra seat every 3.8 groups ($45.60 ÷ $12). After two quarters, compare Plus hosts with similar Group-ready hosts. If Plus hosts are not filling at least that many extra seats, cut the uplift.

## Sales Leadership: sell the hold first, the upsell second

- **Lead with Group-ready.** A city launches only when 60% of its vetted hosts opt in. At 60%, a group comparing three options has at least one group-bookable choice 94% of the time (1 − 0.4³); at 40% it drops to 78%. Sell Plus only after a host has seen group bookings; reps earn commission on Plus only after their city passes the gate.
- **Expect hosts to object to the hold, not the price.** Airbnb launched group split payments with a 72-hour hold in 2017 and retired them in 2018 because hosts disliked holding dates for unconfirmed groups. The answer: the deadline is short, seats come back automatically, and the group books together or not at all.
- **The Plus pitch:** "Four points, on group bookings only. One extra seat is worth $45.60 to you. Plus pays for itself if it fills one extra seat in roughly every four groups." 24% is well under the 30 to 35% operators report paying Viator under Accelerate.
- **Start with hosts who have unsold mid-week capacity.** A filled seat is worth the most to them.

## Product Marketing: travelers never see a price

- **Checkout has no Roamly fee, no tier and no choice.** Keep it that way on every surface.
- **Organizer message:** never be the bank for the group trip again. **Friend message:** one tap and your seat is yours.
- **Do not position against Viator on price.** The claim is that the group books together or not at all.
- **Pitch Plus to hosts as filled capacity,** "a full group instead of two stray seats," not as visibility.

## Rollout: a pricing change is a launch

- **Existing hosts:** nothing changes unless they opt in, so no grandfathering is needed. Plus and Private Groups apply to group bookings only.
- **Localization:** the commission is a percentage and travels across the 40 cities. The group price for parties of 6+ is set by each host in local currency, since local purchasing power, not exchange rates, decides what a group will pay.
- **Monitoring dashboard, live before launch:** host opt-in and retention per city, group completion rate, holds released unpaid, holds a host releases before the deadline, and Plus uptake against Group-ready opt-in. If Plus uptake pulls Group-ready opt-in below 60% in a city, pause Plus there.

## Validate first: will hosts keep holding seats once they have lived with it?

If hosts will not hold seats, there is no product to price. The 60% gate tests whether hosts sign up. Airbnb's hosts did not quit at sign-up; they quit after living with holds. So test both:

1. **Sign-up.** In the first launch city, sales asks vetted hosts with unsold mid-week capacity to opt into Group-ready on the real hold terms. Pass: at least 60%. Below that, the city does not launch.
2. **Staying.** Run real group bookings through those hosts for one quarter. Track the share still opted in (below 60%, the city fails the gate it launched on) and holds a host releases before the deadline, each of which breaks the group's promise. Set that threshold before the pilot starts.
3. **Then** run Van Westendorp on the Plus uplift, with hosts who have seen group bookings. Before that, hosts would be pricing something they have not experienced.
