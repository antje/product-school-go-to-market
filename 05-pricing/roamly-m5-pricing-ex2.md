- Pricing goal: Maximize revenue from the audience Roamly already has, by adding seats to each booking, not by raising any price. The strategic bet is to own the commitment moment for friend groups whose organizer is already a Roamly user. That is a known audience, not a new market, and we are not defending a position on price against Viator, which charges travelers no fee on 300,000 listings. The revenue already sits in the group sessions that start and die today: each completed group of five at $60 a seat is $300 of bookings, and $60 to Roamly at a 20% commission, where today the session earns nothing. It is too early to price the traveler side: group completion is the unproven step, and a price change aimed at people who have not yet reached the value is the wrong lever.
- Pricing model: Transaction-based, on the commission Roamly already takes, charged to the host. Value is event-driven: it exists only when a group books. Rejected: a subscription for organizers (they book a group two or three times a year), seat-based (it would tax the extra seats we want), and a per-seat group fee for travelers (the friend has no pain today and is the most price-sensitive person in the booking; if a fee cut per-friend completion from 95% to 90%, a group of seven would complete 48% of the time instead of 70%; Viator and a payment pool cost the traveler nothing). The organizer and friends are users, get not being the bank, and never see a charge from Roamly. Roamly carries the one new cost: splitting one payment into five adds four card transactions, about $1.20 in fixed fees on a group of five, or 2% of the $60 commission.

Split payment and the hold are in every tier. The hold policy is the same in every tier. Good, Group-ready: standard commission (assumed 20%, parity with Viator's base; Roamly's real rate is an open item); the host accepts the standard group hold and becomes group-bookable; free on purpose because the launch needs 60% host opt-in per city.
