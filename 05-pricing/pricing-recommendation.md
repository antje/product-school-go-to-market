# Pricing & Packaging Recommendation: Roamly Groups

> Module 5 · Increase Revenue Through Smarter Pricing and Packaging · Deliverable 5
>
> A pricing model and packaging structure for a feature with no existing price, worked in the class order: value first, packaging second, research last. Travelers pay nothing extra; hosts pay through the commission Roamly already takes, in three tiers.

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Maximize revenue from the audience Roamly already has, by adding seats to each booking, not by raising any price.
- **Why this is the right commercial goal now:** The Module 2 bet is to own the commitment moment for friend groups whose organizer is already a Roamly user. That is a known audience, not a new market. Competing on price is not an option: Viator charges travelers no fee on 300,000 listings. The revenue already sits in the group sessions that start and die today. A completed group of five at $60 a seat is $300 of bookings and $60 to Roamly at a 20% commission, where today that session earns nothing.

It is also too early to price the traveler side. Group completion is the unproven step, and it is the leading indicator set in Module 1. Pricing people who have not yet reached the value is the wrong lever, so travelers get no price at all.

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** Transaction-based, on the commission Roamly already takes, charged to the host.
- **Who pays, and for what?** The host pays. The traveler pays the listed price with no booking fee.

Value is event-driven: it exists only when a whole group pays, and it arrives in full at that moment. A commission on completed group bookings charges at exactly that point.

The buyer and the users get different value. The host buys filled capacity: a full group instead of two stray seats, and seats back by the deadline instead of no-shows. The organizer and the friends are users. They get not being the bank, and they never see a charge from Roamly.

Rejected alternatives:

- **An organizer subscription.** Organizers book a group two or three times a year, so a subscription would charge for access to a checkout flow.
- **Seat-based pricing.** It would tax the extra seats the product exists to add.
- **A per-seat group fee for travelers.** The friend has no pain today and is the most price-sensitive person in the booking, and a fee lands on every friend. If a fee cut per-friend completion from 95% to 90%, a group of seven would complete 48% of the time instead of 70% (0.90^7 against 0.95^7). Viator and a payment pool both cost the traveler nothing.

Roamly carries the one new cost itself. Splitting one payment into five adds four card transactions, about $1.20 in fixed fees on a group of five, or 2% of the $60 commission.

## 3. Packaging approach

- **Structure:** By features, for hosts only.
- **Audiences & fit:** One structure serves both Module 4 audiences, the organizer and the friend: no tier, no fee, nothing to choose at checkout. Any choice at that moment is friction for the organizer and a reason to leave for the friend. The tiers are for hosts, the party Module 4 reached with a sales line, and what they buy is group demand. Split payment and the hold are in every tier, because every group booking uses them. The hold policy is the same in every tier, so the promise on every surface ("if it doesn't fill by the deadline, nobody is charged") never depends on which host a group booked.

| Tier | Who it's for | Price | What's included |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group-ready | Every vetted host. The launch tier: each city needs 60% of its hosts to opt in. | Standard commission, no uplift (assumed 20%) | Accepts the standard group hold; becomes group-bookable |
| Group Plus ★ | Hosts with unsold mid-week capacity, once they have seen group bookings | 24% on group bookings only (+4 points) | Priority placement in the "booking for a group?" entry point and group search, a group-friendly badge, a group price the host sets for parties of six or more |
| Private Groups | Hosts who can run a whole session for one group | 26% on group bookings only (+6 points) | Everything in Plus, a private-session listing with a minimum group size, a monthly group demand report |

Group Plus is the tier to route hosts toward. On a group of five at $60, the four points cost the host $12. One extra seat is worth $45.60 to the host after commission. So Plus pays for itself if it fills one extra seat every 3.8 groups. At 24%, it also stays well under the 30 to 35% that operators report paying Viator under its Accelerate visibility program, which keeps the supply-side advantage from Module 2.

The graduation path is visible: a Group-ready host sees what their filled groups would have earned on Plus.

## 4. Research to validate

- **Method:** Van Westendorp with vetted hosts in the launch cities, on the Group Plus uplift, asking the four price-perception questions in points of commission on group bookings. It sets the one number we have to choose. Recruit hosts with unsold mid-week capacity, the profile Plus is built for, and run it only after they have seen group bookings, because before that they would be pricing a guess. Then Max-Diff on the Plus and Private Groups benefits (placement, badge, group pricing, private listing, demand report) to decide what sits in which tier. Conjoint can wait: with one number and five benefits, there are no configurations to trade off.
- **Assumption to validate:** That a four-point uplift sits inside the range hosts consider acceptable for the demand Plus brings.
- **The one thing that would change everything if you got it wrong:** That hosts accept the hold at all, and keep accepting it. Every price here assumes seats can be held for a group that has not paid yet. Airbnb launched group split payments with a 72-hour hold in 2017 and retired them in 2018 because hosts disliked holding dates for unconfirmed groups. Its hosts did not refuse at sign-up; they quit after living with holds. If hosts will not hold, there is no all-or-nothing promise and no reason for a group commission; without the hold there is nothing to price.

## 5. The one-page recommendation

> We recommend **a transaction-based commission paid by hosts, packaged as three host tiers with no traveler-facing price** for Roamly Groups, to **maximize revenue from the audience Roamly already has by completing group bookings that die today**, because **value exists only when a whole group pays, the host is the one who gains filled capacity, and any fee at checkout would cut the completion rate the product depends on**.

For each stakeholder, what they most need to hear:

- **Finance** (margin & revenue model): Revenue comes from volume, not a higher rate, and no existing price changes. Per group of five at $60, Roamly earns $60 on Group-ready, $72 on Plus and $78 on Private Groups, where today that session earns $0. The new cost is about $1.20 in card fees per group, leaving $58.80 net on Group-ready. The forecast chain is group sessions started × completion rate × seats × seat price × commission; Roamly's real commission rate and group sessions per launch city are the two inputs to fill before any forecast leaves this page. Rollback: if Plus hosts are not filling one extra seat every 3.8 groups after two quarters, compared with similar Group-ready hosts, cut the uplift.
- **Sales Leadership** (it closes deals): Sell the hold first and the upsell second. A city launches only when 60% of its vetted hosts opt into Group-ready; at 60%, a group comparing three options has at least one group-bookable choice 94% of the time (1 − 0.4³), and at 40% only 78%. Expect hosts to object to the hold, not the price: the answer is a short deadline, seats back automatically, and a group that books together or not at all. The Plus pitch is "four points, on group bookings only, pays for itself at one extra seat in roughly every four groups." Reps earn commission on Plus only after their city passes the gate.
- **Product Marketing** (the story that lands): Travelers never see a price. Checkout has no Roamly fee, no tier and no choice, on every surface. The organizer message is "never be the bank for the group trip again," the friend message "one tap and your seat is yours." Do not position against Viator on price; the claim is that the group books together or not at all. Pitch Plus to hosts as filled capacity, "a full group instead of two stray seats," not as visibility.

Rollout, treated as a launch:

- **Existing hosts:** nothing changes unless they opt in, so no grandfathering is needed. The uplift applies to group bookings only.
- **Localization:** the commission is a percentage and travels across the 40 cities. Hosts set the group price for parties of six or more in local currency, because local purchasing power, not the exchange rate, decides what a group will pay.
- **Monitoring dashboard, live before launch:** host opt-in and retention per city, group completion rate, holds released unpaid, holds a host releases before the deadline, and Plus uptake against Group-ready opt-in. If Plus pulls Group-ready opt-in below 60% in a city, pause Plus there.

**Validate first:** that hosts keep holding seats after living with holds, not only that they sign up. In the first launch city, sales asks vetted hosts with unsold mid-week capacity to opt into Group-ready on the real hold terms; below 60%, the city does not launch. Then run real group bookings through those hosts for one quarter and track two numbers: the share still opted in (below 60%, the city fails the gate it launched on) and holds a host releases before the deadline, each of which breaks the group's promise. That threshold is set before the pilot starts. Only then does the Van Westendorp study run.

**Assumptions to replace with data:** Roamly's commission rate (20%, parity with Viator's base), average seat price ($60, inside the tens-to-a-few-hundred-dollar range from Module 1), and average group size (five, from the segment of 3 to 8 and the at-least-four-seats target from Module 1).
