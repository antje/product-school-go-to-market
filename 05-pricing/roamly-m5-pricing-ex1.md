# Roamly Groups · Pricing & Packaging Recommendation, Module 5 (Exercise 1)

## Responses

### Decision 1 · Pricing goal

**Pricing goal (where to win first)**

Maximize revenue from the audience Roamly already has, by adding seats to each booking, not by raising any price. The M2 bet is to own the commitment moment for friend groups whose organizer is already a Roamly user. That is a known audience, not a new market, and we are not defending a position on price against Viator, which charges travelers no fee on 300,000 listings.

**Why (justification)**

The revenue already sits in the group sessions that start and die today. Each completed group of five at $60 a seat is $300 of bookings, and $60 to Roamly at a 20% commission, where today the session earns nothing. So the goal is to capture that, and the price must not add friction at the one moment the product exists to win. It is also too early to price the traveler side: group completion is the unproven step (it is the leading indicator from Module 1), and a price change aimed at people who have not yet reached the value is the wrong lever.

### Decision 2 · Pricing model

**Pricing model**

Transaction-based, on the commission Roamly already takes, charged to the host. Value is event-driven: it exists only when a group books and is delivered in full at that moment. Rejected: a subscription for organizers, who book a group two or three times a year, so it would charge for access to a checkout flow. Rejected: seat-based, which would tax the extra seats we are trying to add. Rejected: a per-seat group fee for travelers. The friend has no pain today and is the most price-sensitive person in the booking, and a fee lands on every one of them. If a fee cut per-friend completion from 95% to 90%, a group of seven would complete 48% of the time instead of 70%. Viator and a payment pool both cost the traveler nothing.

**Who pays (buyer vs. user)**

The host, and the traveler pays the listed price with no booking fee. Buyer and user are different people and get different value. The host buys filled capacity: a full group instead of two stray seats, and seats back by the deadline instead of no-shows. The organizer and friends are users; they get not being the bank, and they never see a charge from Roamly. Roamly carries the one new cost itself: splitting one payment into five adds four card transactions, about $1.20 in fixed fees on a group of five, or 2% of the $60 commission.

### Decision 3 · Packaging approach

**Packaging (the structure)**

One structure serves both Module 4 audiences: no tier, no fee, nothing to choose at checkout, because any choice at that moment is friction for the organizer and a reason to leave for the friend. The tiers are for hosts, the party Module 4 reached with a sales line, and what they buy is group demand. Split payment and the hold are in every tier, because every group booking uses them. The hold policy is the same in every tier, so the promise on every surface ("if it doesn't fill by the deadline, nobody is charged") never depends on which host you booked.

**Tiers (what each is for)**

- **Good, Group-ready:** standard commission (assumed 20%, parity with Viator's base; Roamly's real rate is an open item). The host accepts the standard group hold and becomes group-bookable. For every vetted host, and free on purpose: the launch needs 60% opt-in per city.
- **Better ★, Group Plus:** +4 points on group bookings only (24%). Priority placement in the "booking for a group?" entry point and group search, a group-friendly badge, and a group price the host sets for parties of six or more. The tier to route hosts toward. On a group of five at $60 the uplift is $12; one extra seat is worth $45.60 to the host, so Plus pays for itself if it fills one extra seat in about every four groups. It also stays well under the 30 to 35% operators report paying Viator under Accelerate.
- **Best, Private Groups:** +6 points (26%). Everything in Plus, plus a private-session listing with a minimum group size and a monthly group demand report. For hosts who can run a whole session for one group.

Graduation path: a Group-ready host sees what their filled groups would have earned on Plus.

### Decision 4 · Research method

**Which research method would you commission, and what specific assumption are you trying to validate?**

Van Westendorp with vetted hosts in the launch cities, on the Group Plus uplift, asking the four questions in points of commission on group bookings. It sets the one number we have to pick. Recruit hosts with unsold mid-week capacity, since they are the ICP for Plus. Then Max-Diff on the Plus and Best benefits (placement, badge, group pricing, private listing, demand report) to decide what sits in which tier. Not conjoint yet: one number and five benefits is not a configuration space.

**What is the one thing in your recommendation that would change everything if you got it wrong?**

That hosts accept the hold at all. Every price above assumes seats can be held for a group that has not paid yet. Airbnb shipped this in 2017 and retired it in 2018 because hosts disliked holding dates for unconfirmed groups. If hosts will not hold, there is no all-or-nothing promise, and no reason for a group commission. The price question disappears because the product does. This is not a pricing survey question: it is validated by the Module 2 launch gate, at least 60% of vetted hosts per city opting into Group-ready before that city launches.
