### Step 1 · Research the competitive landscape

**Prompt**

I am a PM at Roamly, a marketplace that connects travelers to curated local experiences hosted by vetted local experts across 40 cities. My target segment is friend groups of 3 to 8 people planning a shared trip, reached through the organizer: an existing Roamly user who today opens a group chat, a spreadsheet and a payment app to get everyone booked, fronts the money, and chases friends to pay them back. Our value proposition for them is Roamly Groups, a group checkout where every friend pays their own share while Roamly holds the slot, so the booking confirms for everyone at once and nobody is out of pocket. Identify my top 3 competitors, including at least one I might not have considered, and for each, tell me: who they primarily serve, how they position themselves, where they win, and where they are most vulnerable. Also tell me what the status quo alternative is for a buyer who chooses none of them.

Use web search to ground this in what these companies actually offer today, and cite what you found.

**Response**

Here is what the research turned up. Three competitors, one of which is a payments app rather than a marketplace, plus the status quo. Sources are at the end of each section.

#### Framing first

Roamly Groups competes on two axes at once: where the group finds the experience (marketplace) and how the group pays for it (money movement). Nobody today does both well for a 3 to 8 person friend group. Airbnb tried and retreated. Viator solved half of it. The payments apps are creeping toward the other half. That gap is your opening, and it also tells you who closes it first if you do not.

#### 1. Airbnb Experiences (the head-on competitor)

**Who they serve.** Leisure travelers already on Airbnb for a stay, booking a curated local activity as an add-on. Relaunched May 13, 2025 in roughly 650 cities with five categories and an "Originals" celebrity tier. Airbnb paused new experience listings for two years to fix quality and now says it is "not letting just anything" onto the platform. Standard host commission is 20%.

**How they position.** Unique, host-led, vetted, "fitting with the city," with the whole trip (stay, experience, services) inside one app. Group planning is a stated priority: over 80% of Airbnb trips involve multiple people. In May 2024 they shipped shared wishlists with voting and notes, post-booking trip invites, and group messaging with the host. The 2026 summer update adds a shared itinerary that everyone in the party can edit, explicitly pitched as a replacement for the group text thread.

**Where they win.** Distribution and context. If the group is already booking the house on Airbnb, the experience is one tap away, inside the same wishlist the group already voted in. Their vetting story now sounds like yours. Brand trust with the payer is high.

**Where they are most vulnerable.** They do not solve the money. Airbnb launched Split Payments in November 2017 (up to 16 travelers, 72-hour hold, auto-cancel and refund if anyone missed the deadline) and retired it in September 2018. Hosts hated calendar slots being held by unconfirmed groups with no opt-in, and one host-side writeup called it a "failed experiment." Today the organizer books on one card and collects via Venmo or PayPal. None of the 2024 or 2026 group features touch payment. So the exact moment you target, "confirm for everyone, nobody out of pocket," is a moment Airbnb tried, got burned on, and has avoided for eight years. Note the lesson for you as well: the hold cost lands on the supplier, so your experts need an opt-in and a short, predictable hold window or you will repeat Airbnb's mistake.

Sources: [Arival on the relaunch](https://arival.travel/article/inside-airbnb-experiences-relaunch/), [Lodgify on Originals and categories](https://encyclopedia.lodgify.com/airbnb-experiences-relaunch), [TechCrunch May 2024 group features](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/01/airbnb-releases-group-booking-features-as-it-taps-into-ai-for-customer-service), [Gadget Hacks on the 2026 update](https://smartphones.gadgethacks.com/news/airbnb-app-update-2026-ai-planning-group-trips-and-global-support/), [TechCrunch 2017 split pay launch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/28/airbnb-launches-payment-splitting-for-group-trips/), [Spark on the 2018 retirement](https://joinspark.app/blogs/en/can-airbnb-payments-be-split-no-native-option-since-2018-workarounds-for-groups), [LinkedIn: Airbnb kills Group Payments](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/airbnb-kills-group-payments-failed-experiment-stuart-lansdale/), [Supasplit on what Airbnb allows today](https://supasplit.app/blog/how-to-split-airbnb-payment)

#### 2. Viator (with GetYourGuide as its twin)

**Who they serve.** Anyone searching "things to do in [city]." Viator lists 300,000+ tours and activities and is the default inventory layer for the whole category. GetYourGuide is the same play with a European center of gravity.

**How they position.** Breadth, price comparison, and flexibility. Viator's differentiator is Reserve Now & Pay Later: a confirmed reservation with no upfront payment, bookable up to four months ahead, cancel free up to two days before, and auto-pay two days before the experience with a reminder first. GetYourGuide does not offer this. Neither site adds fees on top of the listed price.

**Where they win.** Selection and search. A group that has not picked an activity yet will browse Viator, not you. And Reserve Now & Pay Later already removes half of your organizer's pain: she can lock the slot for eight people with zero money down and no personal cash at risk until 48 hours out.

**Where they are most vulnerable.** The other half. At auto-pay, one card gets charged for the whole group. The organizer still fronts it, still chases friends, still eats the no-show. There is no per-person checkout, no group state, no confirmation that fires only when everyone has paid. Positioning is "long-tail inventory," not "vetted local experts," so a friend group that wants a curated pick has to do the filtering themselves. Viator is the one that could bolt per-person pay onto its existing hold mechanic fastest, so watch it.

Sources: [Viator partner page on Reserve Now & Pay Later](https://partnerresources.viator.com/blog/reservenowpaylater/), [Viator partner help](https://partnerhelp.viator.com/en/articles/202-how-does-reserve-now-pay-later-work), [Finding the Universe comparison](https://www.findingtheuniverse.com/best-tour-booking-sites/), [Travel Tinker comparison](https://thetraveltinker.com/planning/getyourguide-vs-viator/)

#### 3. Cash App Pools and Venmo Groups (the one you may not have considered)

**Who they serve.** Your organizer, already, every day. These are not marketplaces. They are the payment apps she uses to collect from friends, and both have moved from "pay me back" to "collect before we book."

**How they position.** Venmo Groups (rolled out November 2023, "one of our most requested features") tracks shared expenses for up to 30 members, calculates who owes whom, and minimizes the number of settle-up payments. It still assumes one person paid first. Cash App Pools (announced July 29, 2025) goes a step further: the organizer names a pool, sets a goal amount, invites members, and collects contributions, including from non-Cash-App users via Apple Pay and Google Pay, "before the event occurs, so no single person has to front the entire cost." PayPal has a similar Pools product. Partiful's Chip In does the same inside an RSVP flow, routed through Venmo, Cash App or PayPal, and is free.

**Where they win.** Zero switching cost and zero merchant dependence. A pool works for any activity on any site, plus the dinner, the rental car and the Airbnb itself. The organizer does not have to convince seven friends to create Roamly accounts. Network effects are enormous and the friends are already there.

**Where they are most vulnerable.** They collect money but they do not hold inventory. A pool has no idea whether the cooking class still has eight seats. The organizer still has to book somewhere, and if the slot sells out while the pool fills, the money is stuck in her account and the plan dies. There is no atomic "everyone paid, booking confirmed" moment and no refund logic tied to a supplier. That is exactly what Roamly Groups provides, and it only matters if the slot is scarce and the experience is worth waiting for. Also note that Pools launched to a limited set of users in July 2025 with broad rollout "in the coming months"; check current availability before you quote it.

Sources: [Cash App press release on Pools](https://cash.app/press/cash-app-launches-pools-for-group-payments), [Finovate on Pools](https://finovate.com/cash-app-launches-group-payments-feature-called-pools/), [TechCrunch on Venmo Groups](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/14/venmo-gets-a-new-way-to-split-expenses-among-groups-like-clubs-teams-trip-buddies-and-more), [Venmo help: managing group expenses](https://help.venmo.com/cs/articles/managing-expenses-for-venmo-groups-vhel173), [Partiful help: collecting money](https://help.partiful.com/hc/en-us/articles/48252834273179-How-do-I-collect-money-from-guests)

#### Watchlist: SquadTrip

The closest functional analog to your mechanic is SquadTrip: a booking page per trip, each traveler pays their own share by card, Apple Pay or Klarna, installments and auto-retry, a dashboard of who paid, Stripe payouts. It did not make the top three because it serves organizers of retreats, tours and destination weddings (2,000+ organizers, free tier, paid from $29/month, flat 6% processing fee passed to travelers). A friend planning a $60 walking tour will not set up a Stripe-backed booking page. But it proves the per-person-pay-with-hold model works and shows the fee level a market tolerates for it.

Sources: [SquadTrip home](https://www.squadtrip.com/), [SquadTrip payments help](https://help.squadtrip.com/en/articles/12292896-managing-payments-in-squadtrip-payment-methods-installments-and-auto-billing), [SquadTrip fees](https://help.squadtrip.com/en/articles/9794592-payments-and-fees)

#### The status quo alternative

An organizer who picks none of the above does this: proposes options in the group chat, tallies interest in a spreadsheet or a poll, books the experience on her own card (on Airbnb, Viator, or the expert's own site), then sends Venmo requests or a Splitwise entry and chases stragglers for days. Splitwise's free tier now caps logging at roughly 3 to 5 expenses a day with ads, which is why heavy trip planners pay about $4.99/month. Airbnb's own 2017 data said 38% of group travelers had not been paid back what they were owed.

The real status quo, though, is that a large share of these groups never book. The organizer does not want to front $600 for eight people who have not committed, the slot sells out while the chat dithers, and the group "figures it out when we get there." That non-decision is your biggest competitor, and it is also your best message: the reason the organizer books today is not that Roamly is cheaper than Viator, it is that she no longer has to be the bank.

#### Competitive Battlecard: Viator vs Roamly Groups

Segment: friend groups of 3 to 8, reached through the organizer. Last verified September 12, 2026.

#### Know the Landscape

##### Competitor Overview

Viator is the largest experiences marketplace in the category, owned by Tripadvisor, listing 300,000+ tours and activities worldwide. It is the default answer to "things to do in [city]" and gets a large share of its demand from Tripadvisor search. Operators sign up themselves, set their own net rate, availability and cut-off times, and Viator resells. Viator does not curate; it aggregates.

Roamly is in 40 cities with vetted local experts. Viator is in nearly every city with anyone who passes onboarding. In the 40 cities where you overlap, assume Viator lists most of your experts too, or their direct competitors.

##### Key Offerings

- Search and filters across a very long tail, including a Private Tour filter and a group-size filter. Group discounts exist at some operators (sometimes from 6 to 8 people) but there is no site-wide group program or group booking flow.
- Reserve Now & Pay Later: a confirmed reservation with no money down, bookable up to four months ahead, auto-charged two days before the experience with a reminder first, cancellable free until then.
- Cancellation: "Standard" listings refund in full up to 24 hours before start. "Strict" listings need seven days. Free cancellation is not sitewide, and Viator has been called out by Truth in Advertising for implying otherwise.
- Payment: one card per booking. No per-person checkout, no group state, no confirm-when-everyone-pays.
- Post-booking: voucher, operator messaging, Tripadvisor reviews.

##### Pricing

- Traveler: no booking fee on top of the listed price (checked August 2026). Price parity is the norm, so Viator's price usually equals the operator's direct price.
- Operator: 20% base commission. Viator's Accelerate program lets operators bid commission up to 30 to 35% for more visibility, and most operators pay above base. This matters for you: your experts feel Viator's take, and the ones on Accelerate are paying a third of revenue to be seen.
- Roamly reference point: your commission structure is the lever. If you are at or below Viator's base, say so to experts, not to travelers.

Sources: [Viator partner: Reserve Now & Pay Later](https://partnerresources.viator.com/blog/reservenowpaylater/), [Viator partner help](https://partnerhelp.viator.com/en/articles/202-how-does-reserve-now-pay-later-work), [OTA Playbook on commission](https://otaplaybook.com/viator-commission-rate-what-operators-actually-pay/), [TicketingHub supplier guide](https://www.ticketinghub.com/en-US/blog/viator-supplier-faqs), [Truth in Advertising on cancellation claims](https://truthinadvertising.org/articles/viators-free-cancellation-claims/), [AllAboardHub on group booking](https://allaboardhub.com/can-i-use-viator-to-book-activities-for-large-groups-or-private-tours/), [Viator partner pricing](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/pricing/), [Finding the Universe comparison](https://www.findingtheuniverse.com/best-tour-booking-sites/)

#### Understand the Battlefield

##### Key Strengths

1. Selection and discovery. A group that has not chosen an activity yet starts on Viator or Tripadvisor, not on Roamly. You are a destination; they are a search engine.
2. Reserve Now & Pay Later solves half your organizer's problem. She can hold eight seats with zero money down and zero personal risk until 48 hours out. This is the single strongest counter to your pitch and your reps must know it cold.
3. Trust at scale. Tripadvisor reviews, a global brand, a 24-hour refund on most listings, and a support operation that a 40-city company cannot match.
4. Supplier lock-in. Operators depend on Viator demand and many pay Accelerate for it. Your experts may be reluctant to list a group product on Roamly that they cannot offer on Viator.

##### Key Weaknesses

1. One card. At auto-pay, the organizer's card takes the whole charge. She still fronts the money, still chases seven friends, still absorbs no-shows. Reserve Now & Pay Later delays the pain; it does not remove it.
2. No group object. Viator has no notion of a group. Nobody but the organizer sees the booking, the countdown, or who has committed. The friends are invisible to Viator.
3. No curation. Filtering 300,000 listings is the traveler's job. "Vetted local expert" is not a Viator claim and cannot be, given how operators onboard.
4. Cancellation fine print. The 24-hour promise does not hold for "strict" listings and Viator has been publicly criticized for how it markets this. Groups who get burned once remember it.
5. Operator margin pressure. 20% base plus Accelerate leaves experts looking for a channel with a better deal. That is your supply-side wedge.

#### Win the Deal

##### Why You Win

- The booking confirms for everyone at once and nobody is out of pocket. This is the whole pitch. Viator cannot say it, and the last marketplace that tried (Airbnb, 2017 to 2018) pulled the feature after ten months because it hurt suppliers. You win when the organizer has been burned before: Airbnb's own research said 38% of group travelers had not been paid back what they were owed.
- Defensibility: the group checkout is not a payments feature, it is an inventory feature. It only works if the marketplace controls the hold, the expert has opted in, and the hold window is short and predictable. Viator would have to renegotiate hold rules with hundreds of thousands of independent operators. You have 40 cities of vetted experts you can put on one policy. That is the moat, and it is a supply moat, not a code moat.
- Curation matches the buyer. A friend group with one weekend does not want 300 options. It wants three good ones. "Vetted" plus "everyone pays their own share" is a combined message Viator cannot copy without changing how it onboards supply.
- Every friend who pays becomes a Roamly account. Viator sees one traveler per booking; you see all eight. That is your acquisition engine, and it compounds only on your side.

##### Why They Win

Say these out loud internally so nobody is surprised in the field.

- The group has not picked an activity yet. Discovery beats checkout every time, and you are not where discovery happens.
- The city is not one of your 40. Game over.
- The group trusts each other and the organizer has a healthy card. Reserve Now & Pay Later plus a Venmo request is good enough, and it is a habit.
- The experience is cheap. Nobody needs per-person checkout for a $25 walking tour. Your mechanic earns its keep above roughly $60 to $80 a head, where fronting $500 or more feels like a loan.
- Price. If your expert lists the same thing on Viator at the same price and Viator shows more reviews, you lose the head-to-head.
- Refunds. If your hold-and-confirm rule means a stricter cancellation window than Viator's 24 hours, an informed buyer will notice.

##### Why You

One sentence for the rep: "Viator lets you hold the seats; Roamly lets everyone pay for their own seat so you are never the bank."

Three proof points behind it:
1. Per-person checkout with a held slot. Each friend pays their share; the booking confirms when the last one pays.
2. Vetted experts in every city we serve, so the group is choosing among good options, not screening bad ones.
3. Nobody chases anybody. The hold has a deadline, the reminders go from Roamly, and if the group does not fill, everyone who paid is refunded automatically.

#### Handle the Hard Moments

##### Quick Dismiss

When Viator comes up and you have thirty seconds:

"Viator is a great place to find a tour if you are paying for yourself. Roamly Groups is built for the one person in the chat who always ends up paying for everyone. On Viator that person still puts the whole thing on their card two days before and spends the trip collecting. On Roamly, every friend pays their own share, the slot is held, and it confirms for everyone at once."

Do not attack Viator's inventory or brand. You will lose that argument and it makes you sound small.

##### Landmine Questions

Questions to plant that Viator cannot answer well:

1. "When the organizer's card gets charged for eight people, what happens if two of them bail?" (Answer: the organizer eats it or cancels the whole booking.)
2. "Can each person in the group pay for their own spot at checkout?" (No.)
3. "Does anyone besides the organizer see the booking or the countdown?" (No.)
4. "Is the free cancellation actually 24 hours on this listing, or is it one of the strict ones?" (Depends; many are seven days.)
5. "How did this operator get onto the platform, and who checked them?" (Self-serve onboarding; no vetting claim.)
6. For experts: "What commission are you paying Viator once Accelerate is included?" (Often 25 to 35%.)

##### Objection Handling

**"Viator already has Reserve Now & Pay Later. I don't have to pay upfront there either."**
True, and it is a good feature. But the auto-pay still lands on one card two days before the experience. Ask: who is that card? If it is you, you are still the bank, you are just a bank that pays later. Roamly charges each friend for their own seat and never charges you for anyone else.

**"Viator has way more options in this city."**
Agree. If you are still deciding what to do, browse Viator. When you have picked something and need eight people to actually commit, that is where Roamly earns its place. Then offer the honest version: in the cities we cover, our experts are vetted, so the shortlist is shorter on purpose.

**"My friends won't sign up for another app just to pay."**
This one is real. Answer with facts about your flow, not aspirations: how many taps from the shared link to paid, whether guest checkout exists, and whether Apple Pay and Google Pay are supported. If the answer is "they need a full account," fix that before you fight Viator on it. Note that Cash App Pools already accepts non-user contributions via Apple Pay and Google Pay, so the bar is set.

**"What if not everyone pays in time? Do I lose the slot?"**
Yes, the hold has a deadline and the slot is released if the group does not fill. Everyone who paid is refunded automatically. That is deliberate: it is the price of the expert agreeing to hold seats at all. Airbnb's version failed because holds were open-ended and suppliers hated it. Then sell the deadline as a feature: it is the thing that finally makes the group chat decide.

**"Viator has a 24-hour free cancellation. What's yours?"**
Answer with your actual policy. If it is stricter, say so and explain why: held seats for a group cost the expert more than a single seat. If it is equal or better, say that and point to Viator's fine print, where "strict" listings need seven days.

**"Same tour is on Viator at the same price with 400 reviews."**
Do not discount. Reframe on the group outcome: same price, but on Viator one of you is fronting the full amount and collecting for a week. If price parity holds and the buyer is solo, let this one go. It is not your segment.

**"I've never heard of Roamly."**
Fair. Lead with the expert, not the brand: this host, this vetting, this review count, and the fact that eight of you each pay your own way. Trust transfers from the expert to the platform faster than the other way around at 40 cities.
