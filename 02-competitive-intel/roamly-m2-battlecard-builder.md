# Roamly Groups · Competitive Battlecard & Strategic Bet, Module 2

## Primary Competitor

**Name**

Viator (Tripadvisor's tours and activities marketplace; GetYourGuide is its European twin)

**Core offering**

The largest tours-and-activities marketplace: 300,000+ bookable experiences worldwide, with price comparison, free cancellation up to 24 hours on most listings, and Reserve Now & Pay Later, a confirmed reservation with no upfront payment, bookable months ahead and auto-charged two days before the experience.

**Positioning**

Breadth, flexibility and price: "things to do in any city," book now and decide later, no fees on top of the listed price. Long-tail inventory, not curation.

**Primary audience**

Any traveler searching "things to do in [city]," from solo tourists to families and groups, who has not picked an activity yet and wants selection and the ability to lock a slot without committing money.

**Pricing / packaging**

Free for travelers, no booking fee on top of the listed price. Suppliers pay commission per booking. Reserve Now & Pay Later on eligible listings: no charge at booking, automatic charge to one card 48 hours before, free cancellation up to two days before.

**Why they're the biggest threat**

Viator already solves half of our organizer's pain: Reserve Now & Pay Later holds a slot for eight people with no cash at risk until two days before. It has the inventory a group browses before it has picked anything, and per-person payment is one product decision away for a company that already runs a hold mechanic. Airbnb is the bigger name, but it tried group split pay in 2017, killed it in 2018, and has avoided it since. Viator is the one that can close our gap fastest.

## Battlecard

### 1 · Know the Landscape

**Competitor overview**

Viator, owned by Tripadvisor, is the largest experiences marketplace in the category: 300,000+ tours and activities worldwide, the default answer to "things to do in [city]," fed by Tripadvisor search. Operators self-onboard, set their own rates, availability and cut-off times; Viator resells. It aggregates, it does not curate. Positioning is breadth, flexibility and price. In our 40 cities, Viator likely lists many of our experts or their direct competitors.

**Key offerings**

Search and filters across a very long tail, including private-tour and group-size filters; some operators offer group discounts from 6 to 8 people, but there is no group booking flow. Reserve Now & Pay Later: a confirmed reservation with no money down, bookable up to four months ahead, auto-charged to one card two days before the experience, cancellable free until then. Cancellation is 24 hours on "standard" listings and seven days on "strict" ones. One card per booking: no per-person checkout, no group state, no confirm-when-everyone-pays.

**Pricing**

Travelers pay no booking fee on top of the listed price; price parity means Viator's price usually matches the operator's direct price. Operators pay 20% base commission, and the Accelerate program lets them bid up to 30 to 35% for visibility, so many pay above base. Roamly's own commission and group cancellation window are open items, not yet set.

### 2 · Understand the Battlefield

**Key strengths**

1. Discovery: a group that has not chosen an activity starts on Viator or Tripadvisor, not on Roamly. We are a destination; they are a search engine.
2. Reserve Now & Pay Later solves half the organizer's problem: eight seats held with zero money down and zero personal risk until 48 hours out. This is the strongest counter to our pitch.
3. Trust at scale: Tripadvisor reviews, a global brand, a 24-hour refund on most listings, and a support operation a 40-city company cannot match.
4. Supplier lock-in: operators depend on Viator demand and many pay Accelerate for it, so our experts may hesitate to list a group product they cannot offer on Viator.

**Key weaknesses**

1. One card: at auto-pay the organizer's card takes the whole charge. She still fronts the money, still chases seven friends, still absorbs no-shows. Pay Later delays the pain; it does not remove it.
2. No group object: nobody but the organizer sees the booking, the countdown, or who has committed. The friends are invisible to Viator.
3. No curation: filtering 300,000 listings is the traveler's job, and "vetted local expert" is not a claim Viator can make given self-serve onboarding.
4. Cancellation fine print: the 24-hour promise does not hold for strict listings, and Viator has been publicly criticized for how it markets this.
5. Operator margin pressure: 20% base plus Accelerate leaves experts looking for a channel with a better deal. That is our supply-side wedge.

### 3 · Win the Deal

**Why you win**

The booking confirms for everyone at once and nobody is out of pocket. Viator cannot say it, and the last marketplace that tried (Airbnb, 2017 to 2018) pulled the feature after ten months because it hurt suppliers. We win when the organizer has been burned before: Airbnb's own research found 38% of group travelers had not been paid back what they were owed. The advantage is defensible because group checkout is an inventory feature, not a payments feature: it only works if the marketplace controls the hold, the expert has opted in, and the window is short and predictable. Viator would have to renegotiate hold rules with hundreds of thousands of independent operators; we can put 40 cities of vetted experts on one policy. A supply moat, not a code moat. Curation matches the buyer: a friend group with one weekend wants three good options, not 300. And every friend who pays is offered a Roamly account after paying; Viator sees one traveler per booking, we see all eight.

**Why they win**

- The group has not picked an activity yet. Discovery beats checkout, and we are not where discovery happens.
- The city is not one of our 40.
- The group trusts each other and the organizer has a healthy card. Pay Later plus a Venmo request is good enough, and it is a habit.
- The experience is cheap. Nobody needs per-person checkout for a $25 walking tour; the mechanic earns its keep when fronting the total feels like a loan.
- Same tour, same price, and Viator shows 400 reviews. We lose the head-to-head.
- If our hold-and-confirm rule means a stricter cancellation window than Viator's 24 hours, an informed buyer will notice.

**Why you?**

Viator lets you hold the seats; Roamly lets everyone pay for their own seat, so you are never the bank. Three proof points: per-person checkout on a held slot, confirmed when the last friend pays; vetted experts in every city we serve, so the group chooses among good options instead of screening bad ones; nobody chases anybody, because the hold has a deadline, reminders come from Roamly, and if the group does not fill nobody is charged. Over doing nothing: the group that "figures it out when we get there" usually never books, and the reason is that one person did not want to be the bank.

### 4 · Handle the Hard Moments

**Quick dismiss**

"Viator is a great place to find a tour if you are paying for yourself. Roamly Groups is built for the one person in the chat who always ends up paying for everyone. On Viator that person still puts the whole thing on their card two days before and spends the trip collecting. On Roamly, every friend pays their own share, the slot is held, and it confirms for everyone at once." Do not attack Viator's inventory or brand; that argument is lost and it makes us sound small.

**Landmine questions**

1. When the organizer's card gets charged for eight people, what happens if two of them bail?
2. Can each person in the group pay for their own spot at checkout?
3. Does anyone besides the organizer see the booking or the countdown?
4. Is the free cancellation actually 24 hours on this listing, or is it one of the strict ones?
5. How did this operator get onto the platform, and who checked them?
6. For experts: what commission are you paying Viator once Accelerate is included?

**Objection handling**

"Viator already has Reserve Now & Pay Later." True, and it is a good feature. But the auto-pay still lands on one card two days before. Who is that card? If it is you, you are still the bank, just one that pays later. Roamly charges each friend for their own seat and never charges you for anyone else.

"Viator has way more options in this city." Agree. If you are still deciding, browse Viator. When you have picked something and need eight people to commit, that is where Roamly earns its place. In our cities the shortlist is shorter on purpose, because the experts are vetted.

"My friends won't sign up for another app just to pay." This one is real. Answer with the flow: taps from link to paid, guest checkout, Apple Pay and Google Pay. Cash App Pools already accepts non-user contributions, so that is the bar.

"What if not everyone pays in time? Do I lose the slot?" Yes, the hold has a deadline and the slot is released if the group does not fill; nobody is charged, because payments are captured only when the last seat is paid. That is the price of the expert agreeing to hold seats at all, and the deadline is what finally makes the group chat decide.

"What's your cancellation policy?" Answer with the actual policy once set. If stricter than 24 hours, say why: held seats for a group cost the expert more than one seat. And point to Viator's fine print, where strict listings need seven days.

"Same tour on Viator, same price, 400 reviews." Do not discount. Same price, but on Viator one of you is fronting the full amount and collecting for a week. If the buyer is solo, let it go; not our segment.

"I've never heard of Roamly." Fair. Lead with the expert: this host, this vetting, this review count, and eight of you each paying your own way.

## Strategic Bet (Data → Insight → Belief → Bet)

### Data

**Who Roamly Groups is for**

The organizer of a friend group of 3 to 8 who has already picked or shortlisted an experience in one of our 40 cities and now needs the group to commit. Not the browsing group; the decided group. Discovery happens on Viator and Tripadvisor. Commitment is where Roamly Groups lives: the moment one person is about to put the whole thing on their card.

**The one reason to choose it**

The booking confirms for everyone at once and nobody is out of pocket. Each friend pays their own seat while the expert holds the slot. Viator's Reserve Now & Pay Later still charges one card, and a payment pool cannot hold a seat.

**Biggest threat to that position**

Viator bolts per-person payment onto Reserve Now & Pay Later. It already runs the hold; per-person is one product decision away, and it owns discovery. The second threat is on our own supply side: Airbnb shipped this mechanic in 2017 and killed it in 2018 because hosts hated a 72-hour hold they never opted into. If our experts feel the same, the value proposition is false.

### Insight

**The signal that changes the market view**

Viator wins on discovery and on "good enough": hold the seats free, collect on Venmo. So the checkout screen is not the differentiator; Viator can copy a screen. What Viator cannot copy quickly is a supply policy: hundreds of thousands of self-onboarded operators would each have to agree to hold seats for unconfirmed groups, and the last marketplace that imposed that (Airbnb) retreated within a year. Roamly must do differently what Viator structurally cannot: win the experts first. A group hold that every expert in our 40 cities has opted into, with a short deadline and a clear answer to who bears the cost when it releases unpaid, is the moat. The checkout is just how customers see it.

### Belief

I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when the group has already chosen an experience and one person is about to become the bank, because at that moment Viator can only charge one card, a payment pool cannot hold the seat, and only a marketplace whose experts have agreed to hold seats can confirm everyone at once.

### Bet

**I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by**

I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by owning the commitment moment, not the discovery moment: per-seat checkout on a slot that every expert in our 40 cities has opted to hold, with a short deadline, so the booking confirms for everyone at once and the organizer is never the bank. Same segment as M1, doubled down; the change is that the supply-side hold policy becomes the first thing we build and sell, before the customer-facing checkout.

**To make this bet, I am deprioritizing**

To make this bet, I am deprioritizing discovery and breadth: no attempt to out-browse Viator, no inventory push, no group planning features (shared wishlists, voting, itinerary) in the launch, and no launch in cities or with experts who will not opt into the hold policy, even if that shrinks launch supply below the 40 cities. We enter after the group has chosen, or not at all.
