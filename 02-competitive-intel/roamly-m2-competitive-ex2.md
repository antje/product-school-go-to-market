# Roamly Groups · Competitive Battlecard & Strategic Bet, Module 2 (Exercise 2)

Inputs: the Viator battlecard (`roamly-m2-battlecard.md`) and the Module 1 V1 strategy. Same segment as Module 1, doubled down.

## Responses

### Data · What does the intelligence actually show?

**Who is Roamly Groups actually for, based on everything you now know?**

The organizer of a friend group of 3 to 8 who has already picked or shortlisted an experience in one of our 40 cities and now needs the group to commit. Not the browsing group; the decided group. Discovery happens on Viator and Tripadvisor. Commitment is where Roamly Groups lives: the moment one person is about to put the whole thing on their card.

**What is the one thing that makes it worth choosing over the competitor?**

The booking confirms for everyone at once and nobody is out of pocket. Each friend pays their own seat while the expert holds the slot. Viator's Reserve Now & Pay Later still charges one card, and a payment pool cannot hold a seat.

**What is the biggest threat to that position, based on your battlecard?**

Viator bolts per-person payment onto Reserve Now & Pay Later. It already runs the hold; per-person is one product decision away, and it owns discovery. The second threat is on our own supply side: Airbnb shipped this mechanic in 2017 and killed it in 2018 because hosts hated a 72-hour hold they never opted into. If our experts feel the same, the value proposition is false.

### Insight · What changes how you see the market?

**Of all the signals you're seeing, which one changes how you think about the market?**

Viator wins on discovery and on "good enough": hold the seats free, collect on Venmo. So the checkout screen is not the differentiator; Viator can copy a screen. What Viator cannot copy quickly is a supply policy: hundreds of thousands of self-onboarded operators would each have to agree to hold seats for unconfirmed groups, and the last marketplace that imposed that (Airbnb) retreated within a year. Roamly must do differently what Viator structurally cannot: win the experts first. A group hold that every expert in our 40 cities has opted into, with a short deadline and a clear answer to who bears the cost when it releases unpaid, is the moat. The checkout is just how customers see it.

### Belief · What are you now willing to say is true?

**Complete the sentence: "I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when... because..."**

I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when the group has already chosen an experience and one person is about to become the bank, because at that moment Viator can only charge one card, a payment pool cannot hold the seat, and only a marketplace whose experts have agreed to hold seats can confirm everyone at once.

### Bet · What are you committing to?

**"I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by..."**

I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by owning the commitment moment, not the discovery moment: per-seat checkout on a slot that every expert in our 40 cities has opted to hold, with a short deadline, so the booking confirms for everyone at once and the organizer is never the bank. Same segment as M1, doubled down; the change is that the supply-side hold policy becomes the first thing we build and sell, before the customer-facing checkout.

**"To make this bet, I am deprioritizing..."**

To make this bet, I am deprioritizing discovery and breadth: no attempt to out-browse Viator, no inventory push, no group planning features (shared wishlists, voting, itinerary) in the launch, and no launch in cities or with experts who will not opt into the hold policy, even if that shrinks launch supply below the 40 cities. We enter after the group has chosen, or not at all.
